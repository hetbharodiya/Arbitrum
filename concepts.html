Core Web3 Concepts

Mastering the foundational differences that power the decentralized internet.

Web2 vs Web3

Web2 (The Read-Write Web)

Centralized platforms own your data. You create content, but tech giants monetize it. Identity is siloed across different platforms (login with Google/Facebook).

Web3 (The Read-Write-Own Web)

Decentralized networks where you own your data and digital assets. You interact using a universal digital identity (your wallet) without trusting a central authority.

Ethereum vs Bitcoin

Bitcoin (Digital Gold)

A decentralized ledger primarily designed as a peer-to-peer digital currency and a store of value. It has limited scripting capabilities, making it highly secure but less flexible.

Ethereum (World Computer)

A decentralized computing platform that runs smart contracts. It allows developers to build complex decentralized applications (dApps), creating the foundation for DeFi and NFTs.

Public Key vs Private Key

Public Key (Your Address)

Like a bank account number. It's safe to share with anyone so they can send you funds. It is cryptographically derived from your private key.

Private Key (Your Password)

Like the PIN to your bank account. It proves ownership and gives you total control over the funds associated with the public key. Never share it.

Blockchain vs Traditional DB

Traditional Database

Controlled by a single administrator (client-server architecture). Data can be mutated or deleted easily (CRUD operations). Fast and highly scalable but requires trust.

Blockchain

Maintained by a distributed network of nodes (peer-to-peer). Data is append-only and immutable. Operations are slower and more expensive, but trustless and censorship-resistant.
